Joy Multiplied is the new minigame in the [Thanksgiving Event](/guides/thanksgiving-2026), and it's the one people are getting stuck on. It looks like a casual color-matching game, but there's no timer, so it rewards patience. Most failed runs come from placing a stack too quickly and boxing yourself in.

This guide covers how the board works, what each tool does, how attempts are spent, and the placement habits that carry you through the harder stages.

## How it works

You play on a board of hexagon cells. At the bottom of the screen there are **three stacks of colored tiles** waiting to be placed. Drag a stack onto an empty cell, and more stacks appear as you place them.

When a stack lands next to stacks with the same color on top, the matching tiles **merge on their own** into one pile. Once **10 tiles of the same color** sit together in one cell, they clear, you score points, and the cell opens up again.

Each stage has a **point target** shown in a bar at the top of the board. Stage 4, for example, needs 350 points. Reach the target and you clear the stage and earn its reward.

Some cells start **locked**, with a number on them. That number is a points threshold. As your score climbs past it, the cell unlocks and you get more room to work with. Early clears matter for this reason: they don't just count toward the target, they open up the board.

## Stages, rewards and rankings

There are **16 stages**, each with its own clear reward. The early ones pay speedups and resources, and the later ones get much better (Raven Essence, chests and UR hero shards). Each stage card also shows how many players on your server have cleared it, so you can see how far everyone else has gotten. Near the end, those numbers drop off fast.

There's also a **Clear Rankings** board for the players who get furthest, and a **Special Pack** that sells extra attempts and tools. The pack is optional. Free attempts plus careful play get most people a long way.

## Attempts

You get **10 free attempts**. Players report that they refill, so use them instead of letting them sit at 10.

Each run of a stage uses an attempt, and that includes **moving on to the next stage after a clear**. Clearing a stage doesn't give you a free go at the next one, so check your counter before you hit Continue.

## The tools

At the bottom of the board there are three helper tools, each with a limited number of uses:

- **Clear** removes tiles you don't want, which is useful when a stray color is blocking a stack.
- **Move** lets you pick up a stack and put it somewhere else.
- **Refresh** swaps out the three stacks waiting to be placed for new ones.

Save them for when you're actually stuck. A Clear or Move used in the first few turns is one you won't have when the board fills up later.

## Observation Mode

There's an **Observation Mode** switch in the top corner. Turn it on to look inside a stack from top to bottom and count how many tiles of each color it holds. That tells you whether placing the next stack will finish a 10 or leave you a few short.

You **can't move tiles while it's on**, so switch it off again before your next placement. It's easy to forget and think the game has frozen.

## Placement habits that work

**Think about neighbors.** A stack only merges with the stacks touching it. A corner cell has the fewest neighbors, so a stack placed there has fewer ways to grow. Put stacks you want to build into cells with more neighbors, and use the corners for colors you're parking for later.

**Give each color a home.** Try to build toward one color per area instead of scattering the same color across the board. Three stacks of yellow in three different corners won't help each other.

**Check what's underneath.** A stack can have a different color hidden under the top tile. When the top color clears, the hidden one is suddenly in play. Use Observation Mode to check before you commit.

**Count before you place.** If two neighboring stacks already hold 4 yellow each and the next stack has 3, that's a clear. If it only gets you to 9, think about whether that leaves you stuck.

**Always leave room.** The danger isn't running out of moves. It's running out of space for the next three stacks. When the board gets tight, place for space first and points second.

**Take your time.** There's no timer, and there's a **Pause** button. If you're not sure, stop, look at the board and come back to it. Rushing is how most runs end.

<Callout variant="warning" title="Known bug">On opening night, a player reported getting soft-locked after finishing a round, tapping back and making one more move. The game froze and wouldn't restart until they rebooted their phone. Until it's fixed, let each round finish cleanly before you leave the screen.</Callout>

## How BF2 plays it

Use the free attempts every day, and don't buy the Special Pack unless you're chasing the ranking. Play the early stages to learn how the merges behave, and save the tools for later stages where the board gets crowded. Turn on Observation Mode whenever you're not sure what's inside a stack, and turn it off before you move. Slow down. Most of the points in this game come from not making the one bad placement that fills the board.

<Callout variant="tip" title="The short version">10 of one color in a cell clears it. Locked cells open as your score rises. Every stage costs an attempt, including Continue. Place stacks where they have neighbors, check hidden colors with Observation Mode, save Clear, Move and Refresh for emergencies, and take your time. There's no timer.</Callout>
